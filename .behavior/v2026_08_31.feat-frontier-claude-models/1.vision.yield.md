# 1.vision — the brain registry reaches the current frontier

## ⚠️ .headline — the wish's premise correction was itself wrong

the wish told me to verify the model list before i write a single id, and to say so if my read
contradicts it. it does. plainly:

> **a 5-series exists. the issue was right. the wish's correction was stale.**

the wish held that "the most recent claude family known to me is 4.5 / 4.6" and that "the real gap
is the 4.6 pair". a live read of the published docs says otherwise. as of **2026-08-31** the
first-party Claude API lineup is:

| tier | api id | in $/MTok | out $/MTok | ctx | max out | reliable cutoff |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claude Fable 5 | `claude-fable-5` | $10 | $50 | 1M | 128K | Jan 2026 |
| Claude Opus 5 | `claude-opus-5` | $5 | $25 | 1M | 128K | May 2026 |
| Claude Sonnet 5 | `claude-sonnet-5` | $2 | $10 | 1M | 128K | Jan 2026 |
| Claude Haiku 4.5 | `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` | $1 | $5 | 200K | 64K | Feb 2025 |

so the registry does not lag by one minor rung. it lags by **a whole major generation**, and the
gap is wider than either the issue or the wish priced it. three separate surprises fell out of the
verification, each of which reshapes the vision:

1. **three registered slugs are broken on the default client.** `claude/opus/v4`,
   `claude/sonnet/v4`, and `claude/haiku/v3.5` point at models the price page marks **retired
   except on Bedrock and Google Cloud**. the default client is first-party
   (`genBrainAtom.ts:88`), so on the paved path those slugs type-check, ship, and fail at runtime —
   already in `0.4.3`, not a risk we might introduce.
   **but they are not dead for everyone.** `genBrainAtom.ts:86-87` lets a caller inject their own
   client, and an injected Bedrock/Vertex client still reaches all three. so the fix is to **mark
   them, not delete them** (F3) — a deletion would break a config that is correct today.
   ⚠️ two evidence caveats: the runtime-failure claim is **documentary, not empirical** (no live
   call was made against a retired id), and **no test would catch it either way** —
   `genBrainAtom.integration.test.ts:24,27` exercises only `claude/haiku` and `claude/sonnet`, so
   7 of 9 atom slugs, `claude/opus` included, have zero coverage. green CI is silent on them.
2. **a fourth tier exists.** `claude-fable-5` sits *above* opus. the slug scheme
   `claude/{haiku|sonnet|opus}` has no room for it. that is a ubiqlang event, not a config edit.
3. **the structured-outputs contract moved.** `output_format` → `output_config.format`, and the
   beta header is no longer required. the pinned `@anthropic-ai/sdk@0.71.2` is **51 minor versions
   behind** `0.122.0`. the atom's one and only API call is written against the old shape.

the human then settled the version question directly mid-drive: **bump the aliases to v5, and also
expose 4.5 / 4.6 / 4.7 / 4.8 for opt-in.** that settles the issue's headline fulcrum — the alias
moves — and is captured below.

## the outcome world

### day in the life — after

vlad wants a pre-commit PII gate that cannot miss. he writes one line:

```sh
rhx review --brain anthropic/claude/opus --rules 'rules/pii/*.md' --diffs since-staged
```

`claude/opus` maps to `claude-opus-5`. he does not know the model id, does not track the
release cadence, does not edit a config when the next opus ships. he asked for "the best opus" and
got it. the gate has a 1M-token context, so a large staged diff fits in one pass with no chunk
juggle.

six weeks later Anthropic ships opus 5.1. vlad's gate picks it up on his next `npm update`. he did
not act. that is the alias at work.

meanwhile his teammate runs a nightly batch classifier over 40k tickets where cost predictability
beats capability. she writes:

```sh
rhx review --brain anthropic/claude/sonnet/v4.5 ...
```

pinned. her spend per run does not move when the alias does. she opted in.

⚠️ **the rung here was corrected from v4.6 to v4.5 against the verified spec table**, and the reason
is the sharpest ergonomic finding of this vision. her ask is *cost predictability*, and the intuitive
move — "pin the rung just below the newest" — gets her the **worst** of both: sonnet 4.6 is adaptive
with `effort: high` exactly like sonnet 5, so it delivers no relief from the token rise, at $3/$15
against v5's $2/$10. she would pay 50% more for identical behavior.

only **v4.5** is the last extended-thought rung, so only v4.5 answers her actual ask. the cost is
real: v4.5 is 200K/64K, so she gives up the 1M window to get it.

**this is a pit-of-failure the readme must close.** the slug scheme makes "pin one rung back" look
like the conservative choice, and for this caller it is the expensive one. the generated table (F6)
should carry the thought-mode column so the trade is visible at the point of choice, rather than
discovered on an invoice.

### the aha moment

the value clicks at the moment the reader notices **the sonnet rate row went down**.

the instinct behind the issue was "the frontier costs more, so a move of the alias is a breaker we
must warn about." the verified table says the opposite on rates. opus 5 is the **same** $5/$25 as
opus 4.5. sonnet 5 is **cheaper** than sonnet 4.5, at $2/$10 against $3/$15, with 5x the context.

so on **per-token rates** the alias move is same-or-cheaper, with a larger context and newer
knowledge.

⚠️ **but per-token price is not cost-per-call, and i overstated this on first draft.** the 5-gen
uses *adaptive thinking* with a **default `effort` of `high`**, where sonnet/opus 4.5 did no
thinking unless asked. more thinking is more output tokens, and output is the expensive side
($25/MTok on opus, unchanged). so a caller who moves from opus 4.5 to opus 5 pays the same **rate**
and may well pay a larger **bill**.

the honest claim is therefore narrower than "strictly better": the move is **better on capability,
context and knowledge, at an unchanged or lower rate, with a plausible rise in tokens per call.**
that is still a good trade for a gate whose miss is permanent — but it is a real cost change, and it
is exactly the kind of silent shift the issue was right to worry about. the mitigation is that
`metrics.cost.cash` surfaces the token side of that change per call — but only the token side.

⚠️ **and this needs a precision the vision first skipped.** `genBrainAtom.ts:212-215` computes
`cash = calcBrainOutputCost({ for: { tokens: size.tokens }, with: { cost: { cash: config.spec.cost.cash } } })`.
so the reported figure is **real token counts × our own literal rate**. that splits cleanly:

| what changed | does the caller see it? |
|---|---|
| tokens per call rose (the `effort: high` hazard) | ✅ **yes** — token counts come from the API response |
| the vendor's *rate* changed under a stable model id | ❌ **no** — the rate is our hardcoded literal, and no second source contradicts it |

so for **this** move — where the rise is in tokens, not rates — the receipt does its job. but the
figure is not a general-purpose truth teller, and a stale literal yields a confidently wrong number
rather than an obviously missing one. that is the sharp edge behind F5.

**to verify in execution:** run the same prompt through `claude/opus/v4.5` and `claude/opus` and
compare `metrics.size.tokens.output`. if the rise is large, that belongs in the release notes.

that is the whole shape of this work: *the decision everyone feared turned out mostly benign, and
the config file nobody looked at turned out to be broken.*

## user experience

### usecases and goals

| who | goal | reaches for |
|---|---|---|
| gate author | a verdict that blocks a commit; a miss is permanent | `claude/opus` — best available, no pin |
| batch operator | predictable spend across 40k runs | ⚠️ **`claude/sonnet/v4.5`, not v4.6** — corrected against the verified spec table. v4.6 is adaptive with `effort: high`, same as v5, at $3/$15 against v5's $2/$10 — so it costs 50% more for identical behavior. only v4.5 is the last extended-thought rung. the trade: v4.5 gives up the 1M window (200K/64K) |
| repro-chaser | bisect a behavior change across model versions | the full v4.5 → v5 ladder |
| cost-tuner | cheapest thing that still passes the eval | `claude/haiku` |
| agentic task | tool use in a sandbox | `claude/code/opus` |

the two poles are **ride the alias** and **pin the rung**. everything else is a point between them.

### the contract they leverage

unchanged in shape — that is the point. the win is that the same call reaches further:

```ts
import { genBrainAtom } from 'rhachet-brains-anthropic';

const brain = genBrainAtom({ slug: 'claude/opus' });        // -> claude-opus-5
const pinned = genBrainAtom({ slug: 'claude/opus/v4.6' });  // -> claude-opus-4-6

const { output, metrics } = await brain.ask({
  role: { briefs: [] },
  prompt: 'scan this diff for PII',
  schema: { output: z.object({ blockers: z.number(), nitpicks: z.number() }) },
});
```

inputs: `slug` only. outputs: `output`, `calls`, `metrics`, `episode`. the caller's surface does
not grow. the union it can pass grows.

`metrics.cost.cash` is where the alias move shows up — bounded as above (real tokens, our literal
rate), which is enough for this move because the change here is in tokens. a caller who logs it sees the price
change without us having to announce it.

### the timeline they go through

```
t0  npm update rhachet-brains-anthropic
t1  their code stays untouched
t2  next run: claude/opus now hits opus-5
t3  they see a flat-to-lower rate, 5x context — and possibly more tokens per
    call, since the 5-gen thinks by default. metrics.size.tokens shows that rise
t4  (optional) they pin a rung if they wanted the old behavior
```

no migration step. that is the ergonomic bar this has to clear.

## mental model

### how a user describes it to a friend

> "it's a phone book for claude models. you say `claude/opus` and it dials whatever the best opus
> is today. if you need the exact same one every time, you dial the extension — `claude/opus/v4.6`."

### the analogy that fits

**npm dist-tags** — and it matters to get this right, because my first draft reached for `^5` and
that analogy is **false in a way that flatters us**.

a caret range `^4.5` would *never* carry you to 5.x; that is the whole point of a caret. our
`claude/opus` alias **does** cross the major boundary — 4.5 → 5 is exactly the move we make here.
so the honest analogy is not a caret range. it is **`npm install claude-opus@latest`**: a pointer
that moves, with no upper bound.

that matters because it changes the emotional read. `^5` sounds routine and disciplined; `@latest`
in a production dependency sounds reckless to the same JS developer. so the alias-move choice is
**not** "obvious to anyone who knows semver" — it is closer to what semver was invented to prevent.
what makes it defensible here is not the analogy but the facts: same-or-lower rates, a
`metrics.cost.cash` receipt on every call, and a pinned rung one keystroke away.

the analogy still predicts the failure mode: people burned by a pointer that moves are the ones who
never learned pins exist. so **the readme's job is to make the pin visible**, not to keep the alias
timid — and to say plainly that the alias crosses majors.

### their words vs our words

| they say | we say | note |
|---|---|---|
| "model" | slug → model id | one slug, one model id |
| "latest" | alias | the alias moves; say so out loud |
| "version" | rung | 4.5 / 4.6 / 4.7 / 4.8 / 5 |
| "opus" | tier | haiku / sonnet / opus / **fable** |
| "it broke" | the rung retired on the default client | today's actual bug — but live on Bedrock/Vertex |

the term worth guarding: **alias**. anthropic now uses "alias" to mean a pre-4.6 convenience
pointer, and explicitly says a dateless id like `claude-opus-5` is **not** an alias — it is the
pinned snapshot. our `claude/opus` *is* an alias in the moving sense. same word, two senses, one
package. that is `rule.forbid.term.addition.ambiguous` waiting to happen and the readme must
disambiguate.

## evaluation

### how well it solves the goals

| acceptance criterion | how the vision satisfies it |
|---|---|
| ids verified against a citable source | done, below; contradicted the wish and said so |
| frontier reachable from `genBrainAtom` | `claude/opus` → `claude-opus-5` |
| same from `genBrainRepl` | `claude/code/opus` → same config — ⚠️ but gated by the agent-sdk version, see F8 |
| `rhx review --brain anthropic/claude/<slug>` end to end | integration test against the real API — ⚠️ **requires each rung be added to `getBrainAtomsByAnthropic()`**, not just to the union. rhachet discovers only what that function returns |
| readme agrees with the union | **generated from the config** — one source, so agreement is structural (F6) |
| alias decision made and recorded | **made**: it moves, across majors. human-settled, recorded here + in memory |

### pros

- **the alias move is cheap today.** same-or-lower *rate*, 5x context, newer cutoff. no major bump earned. (tokens per call may rise — see the aha caveat.)
- **the ladder becomes honest.** every rung says which platforms still serve it, instead of silence.
- **it surfaces a live bug.** three slugs that fail on the default client get marked rather than left to fail — value the issue never claimed.
- **no caller migration.** the contract shape does not move, and no slug is deleted.

### cons

- **the alias move is silent, and it crosses majors.** a caller who never reads release notes gets a different model — with a plausibly larger token bill, since the 5-gen thinks by default. `metrics.cost.cash` reports it per call, but only to a caller who looks — and only the token half of it (see the aha caveat).
- **scope grew.** the wish framed a config edit. verification turned up an sdk upgrade and a possible new tier. that is real, and it is the wish's own instruction that found it.
- **the back-catalog ages.** every rung we register is a rung we must retire when the vendor does. we now own a lifecycle we did not before.
- **spec figures rot.** price and context change without a model id change (sonnet 5's introductory price *became* the standard price mid-2026). our `spec` freezes them at author time.

### edge cases and the pit of success

| edge case | how the contract keeps them safe |
|---|---|
| caller wants stability | pinned rung slugs exist and are documented next to the alias |
| caller pins a rung retired on the default client | the slug stays (a Bedrock caller still needs it) but carries a `deprecated` note and a readme caveat — an honest label beats both a silent failure and a deletion (F3) |
| the ladder is asymmetric | **sonnet 4.7 and 4.8 do not exist.** the union must not offer `claude/sonnet/v4.7` just to fill the grid |
| tokenizer changed at 4.7 | ~30% more tokens for the same text; a token-budget comparison across the 4.6/4.7 line is apples-to-oranges |
| haiku continuation guard | guards on `config.model.includes('haiku')` — a string sniff that silently matches no id for `claude-fable-5` |
| `max_tokens: 16384` hardcoded | legal on every model, but caps the frontier's 128K headroom at 12% |
| readme drifts from union | it cannot — the table is generated from the config, so there is one source (F6) |
| a registered slug reaches no live model at all | the residual clamp: probe every registered slug against a live model, red before / green after |

the asymmetric-ladder one is the sharpest pit-of-success trap here. a `Record<Slug, Config>` makes
the grid *look* like a product of {tier} × {rung}, and it is not. the type must encode the real
ladder, not the cartesian one.

## open questions & assumptions

### assumptions made

1. ~~this package targets the **first-party Claude API** only, not Bedrock / Vertex.~~
   **weakened on review.** `genBrainAtom.ts:86-88` reads
   `(context?.anthropic as Anthropic | undefined) ?? new Anthropic({ apiKey })` — a caller **may
   inject their own client**, and an injected client can point at Bedrock or Vertex, where opus 4,
   sonnet 4 and haiku 3.5 are **still served**. so "those slugs are dead" holds only for the
   *default* client. F3 must reckon with this: a drop is not purely a fix, it also removes a
   configuration that works for a Bedrock-backed caller today. see F3.
2. ~~the `spec` figures are advisory — consumed for cost estimates, not for billing, so approximate
   is acceptable.~~
   **half confirmed, and the inference i drew was backwards.** the mechanism is exactly as assumed:
   `genBrainAtom.ts:212-215` and `genBrainRepl.ts:323-325` both feed `spec.cost.cash` into
   `calcBrainOutputCost`, and grep finds **no other consumer** — no vendor-reported cost is ever
   cross-checked against it.
   but "not for billing" does **not** imply "approximate is fine". because our literal is the *only*
   rate source, a stale figure does not degrade into a visibly fuzzy number — it yields a
   **confidently wrong** one, with no second source to contradict it. the assumption held; the
   comfort i took from it did not. this raises F5's urgency rather than lowering it.
   the surviving half stands: approximate `gain.grades` are acceptable, wrong **model ids** are not.
3. the human's "bump to v5, expose 4.5–4.8 for optin" settles the alias fulcrum. recorded.
4. ~~`rhx review --brain` looks a slug up through this package's registry unchanged.~~
   **no longer an assumption — verified, and it changed the plan.** rhachet collects only what
   `getBrainAtomsByAnthropic()` / `getBrainReplsByAnthropic()` *return*
   (`asBrainsFromPackageExports.js:17-25`). so the union is necessary but **not sufficient**: every
   rung the human wants opt-in-able must also be added to those two arrays, or it stays invisible
   to the CLI. this is now a hard requirement of the work, not a detail.

### the three that surfaced at the council itself

the enumeration i brought was incomplete. three questions only appeared once the human's rulings
made them askable, and each is a real fork rather than a detail:

- **F13 — does v5 get pinned slugs, or only the alias?** the opt-in ladder existed to give a caller
  a rung to hold. but a ladder that stops at 4.8 leaves the *current* frontier unpinnable: the only
  route to v5 would be the alias, which moves. so a caller who wants "today's best, held still" had
  no slug — the exact stability gap the ladder was built to close, reopened at the top.
  **ruled: pin v5 too.** the ladder now covers every live rung, alias included.
- **F14 — where does the bare `claude/code` point?** it maps to sonnet today. left unexamined, a
  "latest of all" sweep could have been read as "point the bare default at the strongest tier",
  which would have moved every caller who never chose a variant onto a 5x rate.
  **ruled: keep it on sonnet.** and the outcome is strictly good news — sonnet 5 is *cheaper* than
  sonnet 4.5 ($2/$10 against $3/$15) with 5x the context.
- **F15 — do we register Mythos 5?** "latest of all" arguably reaches it. but it sits behind a
  limited-availability gate we cannot confirm our credentials pass.
  **ruled: skip it.** a slug that fails at runtime is precisely the defect F3 exists to clean up —
  we would have shipped a fresh instance of the bug we came to fix.

**the governing principle the human stated: "update to the latest of all."** every tier goes to its
newest rung — haiku is already there at 4.5, so it needs no change — and the back-catalog stays
reachable by pinned slug for opt-in.

#### 🚩 F3 — reversed from "drop" to "mark", on a counterexample i had missed

my first guess was: drop the three retired slugs, accept the breaker, take a major bump. the whole
argument rested on *"they fail at runtime, so they are worse than absent"*.

that argument assumed **every caller uses the default client.** they do not.
`genBrainAtom.ts:86-88`:

```ts
const anthropic =
  (context?.anthropic as Anthropic | undefined) ??
  new Anthropic({ apiKey: process.env.ANTHROPIC_API_KEY });
```

a caller may **inject their own client**. an injected client can point at Amazon Bedrock or Google
Cloud — and the price page marks opus 4, sonnet 4 and haiku 3.5 as *"retired, **except on Bedrock
and Google Cloud**"*. so for a Bedrock-backed caller those three slugs still serve **today**, and a
drop would break a configuration that is currently correct.

so the honest position flips:

| | drop | mark |
|---|---|---|
| first-party caller | helped — a loud compile error instead of a runtime one | unhelped, still fails at runtime |
| Bedrock/Vertex caller | **broken** — a correct config removed | unaffected |
| reversibility | poor once consumers re-pin | trivial |
| version bump | major | minor |

**new best guess: keep the three slugs, and mark them** — a `deprecated: { since, reason }` note
(the F11 field), a readme column that says *retired on the first-party API; reachable only via an
injected Bedrock/Vertex client*, and no type change. that serves the first-party caller with an
honest doc, keeps the Bedrock caller whole, needs no major bump, and stays trivially reversible.

the recorded version posture said *"drop a rung once it retires on the first-party Claude API"*.
that posture was written before i knew about client injection, so it **has been amended** (not
merely flagged) to read **drop only once a rung retires on every reachable platform** — otherwise
"retired" is a statement about one endpoint, not about the model. the amended note also records
that a rung retired first-party but live on Bedrock should be *marked deprecated, not deleted*.

this also removes the item most likely to earn a block, so the road ahead is clearer than it was.

#### 🚩 F6 — the acceptance criterion asks for agreement, and i had reached for a test

the wish says: *"the readme table agrees with the shipped union — the issue notes they are compared

**and the wish had already named the alternative.** its open-questions list asks: *"is the

but a clamp is a **drift detector**, and drift only exists because there are two sources. the table
is a hand-copy of `CONFIG_BY_ATOM_SLUG`; every row is derivable from it — slug, model id, price,

this matters more now than it did at `0.4.3`: the table goes from 9 atom rows to roughly 15, and

section needs a generator, a check that it is current, and a contributor convention — which is its
own small machinery, and arguably more moving parts than one test. if the human prefers the clamp,
that is a clean rework in either direction.

i will best-guess **generate**, and keep a clamp only for what generation cannot cover: that every

### 🚩 F11 — the deprecation path the wish asked about, which my first draft skipped entirely

the wish's third open question was: *"does a slug scheme whose alias moves want a deprecation
path?"* i never answered it. that is a straight omission, not a judgment call.

the answer matters more now than at `0.4.3`, because this behavior creates **two** kinds of
deprecation at once:

1. **rungs that die** — `claude/opus/v4`, `claude/sonnet/v4`, `claude/haiku/v3.5` retired upstream

2. **rungs that fall behind** — opus 4.5 and 4.6 are live but no longer newest. a consumer pinned

### 🚩 F10 — does the opt-in ladder apply to repls too? the extant ladder gives no precedent

the human said "expose 4-5, 4-6, 4-7, and 4-8 / for optin". he did not say whether that means atom
slugs, repl slugs, or both. i looked to the extant repl ladder for the convention and found **it is
already inconsistent with the atom ladder**:

| atom slug | matched repl slug? |
|---|---|
| `claude/haiku/v3.5` | ❌ absent |
| `claude/haiku/v4.5` | ✅ `claude/code/haiku/v4.5` |
| `claude/sonnet/v4` | ✅ `claude/code/sonnet/v4` |
| `claude/sonnet/v4.5` | ✅ `claude/code/sonnet/v4.5` |
| `claude/opus/v4` | ❌ absent |
| `claude/opus/v4.5` | ✅ `claude/code/opus/v4.5` |

sonnet v4 has a repl twin; opus v4 does not. haiku v4.5 has one; haiku v3.5 does not. there is no
rule here to follow — the gaps look incidental rather than intended.

so this is a genuine fork with no precedent to defer to. best guess: **mirror the ladder fully** —
every atom rung gets a `claude/code/*` twin. reasons: the repl config is a one-line delegation per
slug, so the marginal cost is near zero; a full mirror is a rule a contributor can follow, where
today's partial mirror is a set of exceptions; and it removes a class of "why does that one not
exist" questions.

the counter: it doubles the slug count for a repl path whose value is secondary (see F9), and every
slug is a readme row and a retirement obligation.

**that doubled count now has a measured cost, not a feared one.** rhachet *calls*
`getBrainAtomsByAnthropic()` at discovery (`asBrainsFromPackageExports.js:25-29`), so a longer
ladder means more `genBrainAtom` invocations per discovery. i checked what one costs:
`genBrainAtom.ts:47-56` is a `CONFIG_BY_ATOM_SLUG` lookup plus a `BrainAtom` construction, and the
`Anthropic` client is built lazily **inside `ask`** at line 86-88. so registration needs no API key,
touches no network, and costs one object per slug.

that retires the performance and credential objections to a full mirror. what remains is genuinely
a matter of taste and upkeep — a readme row and a retirement obligation per slug — which is exactly
the kind of call that belongs with the human rather than with me.

clean rework — slugs are additive and removable. flagged for the council.

### 🚩 F9 — sequence the work so the atom path never waits on the repl path

surfaced on requirement review. the wish's `.why` argues for **atoms**, explicitly:

> two properties make the API brains the right shape there, as against the repl brains: an API
> brain has **no filesystem**, so it cannot be handed a path outside a sandbox and stall to ask for
> permission; and `rhx review` already carries the numeric-count output contract.

so the motivated usecase — the gate that cannot miss — runs on `genBrainAtom`. the repl half of the
acceptance list is there for symmetry and for other consumers.

but the repl half is where **all the version risk lives**: the two-minor-generation
`claude-agent-sdk` jump (F8) and the silent cost-misattribution hazard. the atom half needs only a
config edit plus a narrow `output_config` call-site change (F4).

**so the most expensive, riskiest requirement serves the least-motivated usecase.** that is an
argument about order, not about scope:

1. **atoms first** — config, unions, registration, `output_config`, readme. this alone satisfies
   the wish's `.why` and unblocks the downstream PII gate.
2. **repls second** — verify `claude/code/opus` against the 0.1.76 agent-sdk with one real call;
   upgrade only if it fails.

if F8 turns ugly, step 1 still ships and still delivers the value the wish was written for. best
guess: sequence it this way. clean rework — it is an order, not a boundary.

## what is awkward?

**1. the version scheme now lies about what a version is.**
our slug says `claude/opus/v4.6`; the vendor's id says `claude-opus-4-6`. fine. but `claude/opus/v5`
maps to `claude-opus-5`, and the vendor calls *that* a pinned snapshot while calling
`claude-sonnet-4-5` (no date) an alias. so "dateless" means *moving* below 4.6 and *pinned* at 4.6
and above. our scheme flattens both into "a rung", which is the right call for our callers but
means our word and their word diverge. it will bite someone who reads both docs at once.

**2. the grid is not a grid.**
`Record<Slug, Config>` invites the reader to see {haiku, sonnet, opus} × {v4.5, v4.6, v4.7, v4.8,
v5}. the truth is ragged: haiku stops at 4.5, sonnet skips 4.7 and 4.8, opus has every rung. the
type will encode the ragged truth, but the *shape* of the file will keep suggesting the grid, and
every future contributor will want to fill the holes.

**3a. "the frontier" assumes a total order that the lineup does not have.**
R1 says reach *the* frontier — singular. but the vendor names Fable 5 as "highest available
capability" **and** Opus 5 as the pick for complex agentic work, at half the price. those are two
different maxima on two different axes. so "the frontier" is not one model, and a caller who asks
for "the best" is asking a question the lineup declines to answer. our slug scheme inherits that:
`claude/opus` can mean "newest opus" precisely because it is scoped to a tier, but there is no
honest slug for "the best available", and i do not think we should invent one.

**3. "fable" breaks the mental model, not just the union.**
haiku/sonnet/opus is a legible ladder — fast/balanced/capable. a caller can reason about it. "fable"
sits above opus at 2x the price and carries no position in that metaphor. worse, there is a "mythos"
behind a limited-availability gate. the tier vocabulary has stopped being an ordered ladder and
become a set of names, and `spec.gain.grades`-driven brain selection assumes an order.

~~i am deferring this (F2)~~ — **stale, corrected on review.** F2 reversed to *register it*, so this
awkwardness is not deferred, it is **accepted**: we take a tier that breaks the ladder metaphor
because the wish's `.why` asks for the strongest model. that is a deliberate trade, not a punt, and
it is still the piece that feels most forced. if the human declines F2 at the council, this
awkwardness goes away with it.

**4. the spec freezes figures that move.**
sonnet 5's price changed *without a model id change* — an introductory rate became standard. our
`spec.cost.cash` is a literal in a `.ts` file. we are a cache with no invalidation, and the readme
table is a cache of *that* cache.

~~the clamp (F6) keeps layer 2 honest with layer 1~~ — **stale, corrected on review.** F6 reframed
from *clamp* to *generate the table from the config*, which does better than a guard: it deletes
layer 2 entirely, so no second cache is left to drift.

but the deeper asymmetry survives that fix and is **sharper than i first wrote**. no guard keeps
layer 1 honest with the vendor — and a read of `genBrainAtom.ts:212-215` shows `spec.cost.cash` is
the **sole** input to every reported `metrics.cost.cash`, cross-checked against no second source.
so a stale literal does not yield a fuzzy number, it yields a confident wrong one.
